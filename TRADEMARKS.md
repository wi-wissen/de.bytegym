## What this means in practice

**You may**, without asking:

- use, modify and redistribute the code, including commercially, under the MIT terms
- fork the project and publish your fork **under a different name**
- state factually that your project is "based on ByteGym" or "a fork of ByteGym"

**You may not**, without written permission:

- call your fork, app, or product "ByteGym", or use a name confusingly similar to it
- publish a modified build to an app store under the ByteGym name

The point is simply that users should always know whose build they are installing. If you
build something on top of ByteGym, give it your own name — the code is yours to use.
